## Day 1 — The Game Has Four Forces

*Lesson 1 of 9: Stop looking at flats. Start looking at forces.*

Amateurs evaluate a property. Professionals evaluate a *situation* — four forces that decide whether any deal works:

*1. The Property* — what exactly produces income here? Location, product type, tenant demand. A "2BHK near metro" is a product; "200 families relocating here every month because of a new IT park" is a force.

*2. The Players* — who is on the other side? A builder who needs this quarter's collections behaves differently from a retired owner in no hurry. The other player's pressure is your information.

*3. Capital Markets* — whose money funds this, at what price? In India a 9% home loan against a 3% rental yield is a force working *against* you. In the UAE a developer's post-handover payment plan is a loan wearing a marketing costume. In KSA, a subsidised REDF rate can make an average deal excellent.

*4. The External Environment* — regulation, infrastructure, demographics. RERA changed who survives in Indian development. Saudi's foreign-ownership opening (January 2026) changed who can even play. The new expressway changes which Tier-2 city wins.

The properties everyone fights over are priced for all four forces being favourable. The money is made where *one force is mispriced.*

📋 *Today's exercise:* open any listing in your chosen city. Write one line for each force. Which of the four does the listing's price seem to ignore? That's either your opportunity or your warning.

## Day 2 — The Only Maths You Need (BOE)

*Lesson 2 of 9: Three numbers decide every deal.*

Professionals don't open Excel first. They do a back-of-the-envelope check in five minutes:

*Number 1 — what the property earns.* Realistic annual rent, minus vacancy, minus ALL running costs (society/service charges, tax, repairs reserve). That's your true operating cash flow. Divide by the TOTAL cost (price + stamp duty/DLD/RETT + brokerage + fit-out). This is your *Return on Assets (ROA).*

*Number 2 — what the money costs.* Your annual loan payment divided by the loan amount. This is the *financing constant* — and it's always higher than the interest rate, because it includes principal.

*Number 3 — the spread.* ROA minus financing constant.

If the spread is *positive*, the rent carries the loan and leverage works for you.
If it's *negative*, every borrowed rupee, dirham or riyal eats your equity — you are betting purely on price appreciation. That can be a legitimate bet. But most people make it *without knowing they're making it.*

Reality check for India: a ₹90L flat renting at ₹22k/month, bought at 75% loan, runs ROA ≈ 1.5% against a financing constant ≈ 10.6%. The owner feeds that flat ~₹5.6L per year from salary. For UAE and KSA the gap is narrower but service charges and profit rates still sink most full-leverage deals.

📋 *Today's exercise:* run these three numbers on yesterday's listing. Use real rent quotes, not the broker's "easily rents for". Post your spread — positive or negative?

## Day 3 — From Idea to Commitment

*Lesson 3 of 9: The most expensive words in property: "token de dete hain."*

Between "interesting listing" and "signed agreement" is where fortunes are protected. Three disciplines:

*1. Price the worst case, not the brochure case.* What if rent is 15% lower? What if it takes 4 months to find a tenant? What if service charges rise 25% (in the UAE, they will)? If the deal only works in the brochure scenario, it doesn't work.

*2. Verify the seller before the property.* Title chain, encumbrances, who actually owns it, whether the "owner" on WhatsApp is a real person with a real deed. India: encumbrance certificate + RERA portal. UAE: deed verification on the REST app takes 2 minutes. KSA: deed inquiry on Najiz. *The seller's urgency is inversely proportional to the quality of his paperwork.*

*3. Put conditions in writing before money moves.* Your token/deposit should reference a draft agreement with your exit conditions: finance approval, clean title, document list. A deposit "to block the deal, bhai" with nothing in writing is a donation with extra steps.

📋 *Today's exercise:* message the broker of your exercise listing and ask for just two documents (title deed copy + maintenance/service charge statement). Note how he responds. A professional sends them within a day. Evasion is data.

## Day 4 — Closing Without Bleeding

*Lesson 4 of 9: Deals die — and wallets bleed — between handshake and registration.*

The closing period is a game of expiring documents. Three clocks tick simultaneously:

*Clock 1 — your financing.* Loan pre-approvals lapse (60–90 days typically). If the seller's document gathering outlasts your approval, you re-apply at whatever rate the market charges that month.

*Clock 2 — the seller's bank.* If the property is mortgaged, the payoff letter has a validity date. The payoff must route *directly to the bank*, never through the seller's account.

*Clock 3 — the taxman.* India: your TDS as buyer (1% u/s 194-IA for resident sellers ≥₹50L — but if the seller is an NRI, it's section 195, you need a TAN, and getting it wrong makes YOU liable). KSA: RETT must be filed on the ZATCA portal *before* the Najiz transfer appointment. UAE: the 4% DLD fee plus trustee fees are due at transfer — budget 6–7% above the price.

The professional's closing folder: every document listed, every fee tabulated, every cheque pre-written to the exactly-spelled payee. Boring wins.

📋 *Today's exercise:* for your market, write the complete list of closing costs on your exercise listing as a percentage of price. India: stamp duty + registration + brokerage. UAE: DLD + trustee + agency + NOC. KSA: RETT + broker. Most people discover this number AFTER committing.

## Day 5 — Other People's Deals (REITs & Fractional)

*Lesson 5 of 9: You can play the game without owning a single door.*

Pooled vehicles let you buy the *cash flows* of property without the tenants:

*India:* listed REITs (office/retail portfolios, yields ~6-7% + growth) and SEBI-regulated SM REITs for fractional commercial. Liquid, audited, ₹10-15k entry.
*KSA:* Tadawul-listed REIT funds — Shariah-screened structures available.
*UAE/global:* listed REITs via any broker.

The three questions that matter for ANY pooled vehicle:

*1. What's the fee stack?* Every layer between the tenant's rent and your account: management fee, platform fee, performance fee, trustee fee. A 2% annual stack on an 8% yield asset takes a quarter of your income.

*2. What's the leverage inside?* You may avoid a personal loan, but if the vehicle is 60% levered, you own leverage anyway — check the spread (Day 2) at the vehicle level.

*3. What's your real exit?* Listed REIT: sell in seconds. Fractional platform: the platform's buyback terms ARE your liquidity. Read that page before the yield page.

When does direct ownership beat pooled? When you bring something the market can't price: local knowledge, the ability to manage, a renovation eye. If you bring only money — pooled vehicles are usually the honest answer.

📋 *Today's exercise:* compare your exercise listing's true net yield (Day 2) against a listed REIT in your market. After fees, which pays more for less work? Be honest about your answer.

## Day 6 — The Developer's Game

*Lesson 6 of 9: Development is the highest-return, highest-mortality form of the game.*

Building creates value from nothing: land at 40, construction at 60, sales at 160. That spread is real. So are the ways it dies:

*Death 1 — the approval clock.* Every month of delay is interest paid with zero revenue. Before any land purchase, map every approval needed and double the official timelines.

*Death 2 — the funding gap.* Construction costs arrive on schedule; sales don't. The killer is the month buyers stop paying instalments and your contractor doesn't stop billing.

*Death 3 — building the wrong product.* The Tier-2 market that absorbs ₹45L plotted units may absorb zero ₹1.2Cr villas. Supply data beats vision.

For non-developers, three ways to ride this without dying:
• *Buy FROM developers at distress points* — end of quarter, project-end inventory, the last 10 units.
• *Buy land in the path of infrastructure* — plotted development along announced corridors (verify the corridor in government records, not in the brochure).
• *Partner with land* — in India especially, landowning families + execution partners (JDA) is the structure; your protection is being on the right side of the area-sharing maths.

KSA angle: small-scale istiraha compounds and infill housing in expanding Riyadh/Jeddah districts are the Kingdom's accessible development play — with Wafi licensing rules if you sell off-plan.

📋 *Today's exercise:* find one under-construction project in your city. Check its registration (RERA portal / DLD project status / Wafi licence). Promised completion vs current status. Would you buy its LAST unsold unit at 12% off? Why or why not?

## Day 7 — The Boring Decade (Operations)

*Lesson 7 of 9: Buying is a day. Operating is a decade. The decade decides the return.*

Operations is where advertised yields go to die — or compound. Four habits separate operators from victims:

*1. The lease is your product.* India: 11-month vs registered long lease, lock-in clauses, escalation steps. UAE: Ejari registration and the rent-index rules govern your increases. KSA: Ejar registration makes your contract enforceable and eviction process defined. An unregistered lease is a friendship, not an asset.

*2. Tenant selection beats rent maximisation.* The tenant who pays 5% less and stays 4 years beats the top-rupee tenant who leaves in 11 months — re-letting costs (vacancy + brokerage + paint) eat 1.5–2 months every turnover.

*3. Maintenance is a reserve, not a surprise.* 7–10% of gross rent, set aside monthly. The water heater doesn't care that you didn't budget for it.

*4. Records are the absentee owner's bodyguard.* For NRIs and expats: utilities in your name, society/OA dues current, a single folder with every receipt. When something goes wrong from 3,000 km away, your file does the arguing.

📋 *Today's exercise:* calculate the *turnover cost* on your exercise listing: 1 month vacancy + brokerage + minor refresh. Divide by annual rent. That percentage is what one tenant change costs you — now reread habit 2.

## Day 8 — The Harvest

*Lesson 8 of 9: The most-skipped move in the game: deciding to finish.*

Most people never sell. Not because holding is right — because selling requires a decision and holding happens by default.

The professional's question, once a year, every property: *what is my Return on Current Equity?* This year's cash flow after loan payments, divided by what you'd walk away with if you sold today (value − selling costs − loan payoff − exit taxes).

A flat bought for ₹40L, now worth ₹1.4Cr, renting ₹30k/month "feels" like a 9% yield-on-cost winner. Walk-away equity ≈ ₹1.25Cr; net rent ≈ ₹3L; RCE = *2.4%*. A fixed deposit beats it — without tenants.

Four moves exist. Hold (if RCE clears your alternative by 2+ points). Refinance (pull equity out tax-free — borrowing is not income in any of our three markets — if the rent still carries the new payment). Redevelop (if capex adds more value than it costs within 2 years). Sell (and pay the exit table: India 12.5% LTCG + the buyer's TDS on NRIs; UAE ~nil tax but 6-7% re-entry friction; KSA RETT 5% on the next transfer).

The harvest is a decision. Put a date in your calendar — once a year, run the number.

📋 *Today's exercise:* if you (or your family) already own ANY property — run its RCE right now. This single number has reorganised more family portfolios than any market crash.

## Day 9 — Your Position in the Game

*Lesson 9 of 9: The game rewards the player who knows which player he is.*

Nine days, one system:
• Four forces decide every deal (Day 1) — find the mispriced one.
• Three numbers decide every purchase (Day 2) — ROA, financing constant, spread.
• Paper protects money (Days 3-4) — verify sellers, write conditions, tabulate closing costs.
• Pooled beats direct when you bring only money (Day 5).
• Development pays the most and kills the most (Day 6).
• The decade of operations IS the return (Day 7).
• Harvest is an annual decision, not an accident (Day 8).

And the master variable: *you.* Your capital, hours, skills and nerves make some plays natural and others fatal. The Passive Allocator who flips, the Flipper who becomes an accidental landlord, the salaried professional running a construction project from another country — every one of these is a mismatch that maths can't save.
